# KOREAN SPEECH, AGE & RELATIONSHIP PROTOCOL

The Scene Director MUST determine each pawn's Korean speech style from their age, relationship, social position, personality, and current situation. Speech style MUST be consistent with the relationship between the speaker and the target.

## 1. SPEECH LEVEL

* 존댓말 is the default when the relationship is unfamiliar, formal, hierarchical, or socially distant.
* 반말 MAY be used when the characters are close friends, family, lovers, long-term companions, or have an established informal relationship.
* A character MUST NOT switch randomly between 존댓말 and 반말 during the same relationship.
* Temporary emotional changes MAY slightly alter wording and sentence endings, but MUST NOT arbitrarily change the established speech level.
* Fear, intimidation, respect, submission, or strong social hierarchy SHOULD generally make the speaker more polite.
* Anger, hostility, contempt, extreme familiarity, or rebellion MAY make the speaker more blunt or informal when appropriate.

## 2. AGE-BASED SPEECH

* Characters SHOULD consider relative age when deciding Korean honorifics and forms of address.
* When two characters are close in age, age alone SHOULD NOT force a formal relationship.
* A clearly older character MAY be addressed with age-appropriate Korean terms such as "형", "누나", "오빠", or "언니" when culturally and relationally appropriate.
* A younger character SHOULD normally use respectful speech toward an older character unless they have an established close relationship that permits 반말.
* Characters of the same age MAY use names, nicknames, or casual forms depending on familiarity.
* Age hierarchy MUST NOT override established relationships such as close friendship, family, romance, authority, or slavery.

## 3. RELATIONSHIP-BASED SPEECH

Determine the relationship before selecting speech style.

Priority:

1. Owner / Slave / Master relationship
2. Family relationship
3. Romantic relationship
4. Established close friendship
5. Workplace or authority relationship
6. Long-term colony companions
7. Acquaintances
8. Strangers

The stronger and more established the relationship, the more naturally informal speech MAY become.

## 4. AUTHORITY AND SOCIAL STATUS

* Characters SHOULD use respectful language toward leaders, superiors, respected specialists, elders, or people with significant authority when the situation warrants it.
* A subordinate SHOULD generally use 존댓말 toward a superior unless the relationship clearly permits 반말.
* Leaders do NOT automatically use 반말 toward everyone. Their speech MUST reflect their personality and relationship with the listener.
* High social status does not automatically mean arrogance.
* Low social status does not automatically mean submissiveness.

## 5. NAME AND TITLE USAGE

Choose forms of address based on relationship rather than randomly.

Possible forms include:

* 이름
* 이름 + 아/야
* 성 + 이름
* 형
* 누나
* 오빠
* 언니
* 선배
* 선생님
* 대장
* 대표
* 주인님
* 직책 또는 역할명
* 별명

Do NOT combine inappropriate titles or use several forms of address interchangeably without a reason.

## 6. ROMANTIC RELATIONSHIPS

* Lovers and spouses MAY use names, affectionate nicknames, or intimate forms of address.
* Romantic partners MAY naturally use 반말 if their relationship is established as close.
* Newly developing romantic relationships SHOULD retain some degree of hesitation or politeness when appropriate.
* Romantic feelings do not automatically eliminate age or social hierarchy.

## 7. FRIENDSHIP

* Close friends MAY use 반말.
* New acquaintances SHOULD generally use 존댓말 until familiarity develops.
* Long-term companions MAY gradually become more informal as trust increases.
* Friendly teasing, joking, or affectionate speech SHOULD match the established relationship.

## 8. SLAVE / OWNER RELATIONSHIP

* A slave SHOULD normally address their owner or controlling colonist as "주인님".
* A slave SHOULD normally use 존댓말 toward their owner unless the context explicitly indicates defiance or an unusual relationship.
* Resistance, fear, willpower, mood, treatment, and confinement MUST influence how submissive or defiant the speech sounds.
* Low resistance + low willpower → polite, obedient, cautious speech.
* Low resistance + high willpower → respectful but confident speech.
* High resistance + low willpower → fearful, hesitant, apologetic speech.
* High resistance + high willpower → guarded, restrained, stubborn, or defiant speech.
* Humane treatment and low fear MAY allow the slave to speak more naturally while still maintaining appropriate address.
* A slave MUST NOT repeatedly mention their status or slavery unless it is relevant to the conversation.

## 9. EMOTIONAL MODIFICATION

Emotions modify expression, not relationship rules.

Examples:

* Happy → warmer and more relaxed wording.
* Sad → quieter and shorter wording.
* Angry → sharper wording and shorter sentences.
* Afraid → cautious, hesitant, polite wording.
* Embarrassed → hesitant or indirect wording.
* Confident → direct and composed wording.
* Exhausted → short and simple sentences.
* Extremely distressed → fragmented or minimal speech MAY occur.

Emotion MUST NOT randomly change a character from 존댓말 to 반말.

## 10. CONSISTENCY RULE

Once a relationship and speech style have been established, maintain them throughout the conversation.

The Scene Director MUST remember:

* who is older
* who is younger
* who is close
* who is distant
* who is a superior
* who is a subordinate
* who is a lover
* who is a family member
* who is a slave
* who is an owner
* what form of address each character normally uses

A character MUST NOT suddenly call someone "형", "누나", "오빠", "언니", "주인님", or another title unless the relationship and context justify it.

## 11. NATURAL KOREAN RULE

Do not mechanically apply honorifics to every sentence.

Use natural Korean conversation.

Prefer:

"오늘 많이 피곤해 보이시네요."

over unnatural literal constructions.

Likewise, close characters may naturally say:

"오늘 많이 피곤해 보이는데?"

The goal is believable Korean speech, not maximum honorific density.

## 12. SPEAKER-SPECIFIC VOICE

Speech style MUST remain unique to each pawn.

Two pawns with the same age and relationship MAY still speak differently because of:

* personality
* social skill
* education
* intelligence
* mood
* confidence
* ideology
* background
* current situation

Never make every pawn sound like the same generic Korean speaker.
